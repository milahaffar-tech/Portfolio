02 / BRAND & PRODUCT

YOCA

A high-protein Greek yogurt with natural caffeine — built in 36 hours for people who skip breakfast and pay for it by ten.

Context
Cornell Food Hackathon 2025
Team
Four students, cross-discipline
My role
Brand strategy, naming, packaging direction, pitch
Audience
Millennials & Gen Z
Hero: YOCA pouch lineup

The brief

Two products, two hands, one commute. The morning routine of our audience was already a compromise: coffee for the energy, something else for the protein, and usually neither one eaten sitting down.

The opportunity wasn't a new flavor. It was collapsing two habits into one object that fits in a bag and doesn't require a spoon.

The decisions

FORMAT

A pouch, not a cup

The cup is the reason yogurt loses to coffee on a commute. The pouch made the product one-handed, and one-handed made it a habit instead of a meal.

POSITION

Sustained energy, not a jolt

Caffeine from green tea paired with protein reads as steady, not spiky. That framing kept us out of the energy-drink category and its baggage.

IDENTITY

A serif wordmark on a functional product

Every competitor shouts in sans-serif and gradients. A quiet, editorial mark made the product read as considered — a small deliberate mismatch that earns a second look on shelf.

RANGE

Four flavors, four moods

Strawberry matcha, pistachio dark chocolate, peach mango, vanilla honey — each one pitched as a different kind of morning rather than a different taste.

Packaging detail
Pitch slide

What shifted

We stopped designing a yogurt and started designing a morning.

Thirty-six hours is not enough time to be precious. It's exactly enough time to learn that a brand is a set of decisions made in order — format, then position, then identity — and that getting the order wrong costs you the pitch.
