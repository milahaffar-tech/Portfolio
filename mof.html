Ministry of Finance

Turning policy material into a client-facing deck that could survive six layers of approval and still hold a room.

Role
Summer intern — communications
Timeline
Summer 2025
Output
Client-facing slide deck
Skills
Narrative structure, information design, stakeholder review
Hero: deck cover or spread

The brief

The material was accurate and unreadable. Everything a client needed to know existed somewhere in the documents — it just arrived in the order the institution produced it, not the order a person understands it.

My job was nominally to build slides. The actual job was to decide what the audience needed at each point of the story: context, then the facts, then a clear reason to act.

What I did

  1. 01

    Rebuilt the spine before the slides

    I wrote the deck as a sentence sequence first — one line per slide — so the argument could be checked without anyone arguing about fonts.

  2. 02

    Gave every figure a plain-language read

    Each chart got a headline stating what it means, not what it shows. The data stayed; the interpretation stopped being optional.

  3. 03

    Designed for the review chain

    A consistent template meant edits from any reviewer landed in one place instead of breaking the layout — the deck could absorb change without losing its shape.

What shifted

The deck went out without a translator standing next to it. That was the point.

I learned that in an institution, clarity is a political achievement as much as a design one. The version that ships is the one every reviewer can see themselves in — so the structure has to be strong enough to hold their edits and simple enough that they trust it.
