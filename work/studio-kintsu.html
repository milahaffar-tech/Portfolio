VI · On growing through the cracks · Product design

Grow Through the Cracks

A lamp for Studio Kintsu that reads kintsugi as a story of growth, not only repair.

From my notebook

Kintsugi says a bowl is more beautiful for having been broken. I wanted my lamp to take that one step further: from healing to growing.

Role
Concept and lamp design
Context
Team project with Perla Imseeh, Mya Khamis, and Emily Norgaard
Output
One lamp in a four-lamp brand
Skills
Concept, form, product story

The brief

Create a lamp brand built around a theme or story. We chose Studio Kintsu, inspired by kintsugi, the Japanese art of repairing broken pottery with gold. The idea: a piece is more beautiful for having been broken.

The brand explores beauty through imperfection, repair, and rebirth. Each of us designed one lamp that tells part of that story.

What I did

I designed “Grow Through the Cracks,” a lamp that reimagines kintsugi as a story of growth.

01

The idea

Floral, biomorphic forms that stand for growth rise from a ceramic base marked by gold fractures. Three floral lampshades stack on top of one another, finished with a spherical topper. The topper is the seed: the moment the cracks have grown into something new.

02

The design, bottom to top

  • Ceramic base with gold fractures: the break, and the repair.
  • Three stacked floral shades: growth rising out of the cracks.
  • Spherical topper: the seed, a rebirth into something new.

The solution

Product story

The lamp is meant to feel hopeful and reassuring. Organic forms and softened light suggest growth after rupture, life continuing rather than being interrupted. It invites people to see repair as a more human way of living, where damage is a starting point for new form instead of an ending.

The brand fit: it pushes Studio Kintsu's idea one step further, from healing to growing.
