03 / SERVICE DESIGN

ReSET Studio

Reimagining the guest experience at the Statler Hotel — for the traveler who checks in already depleted.

Context
Cornell — hospitality & design studio
Partner
The Statler Hotel
My role
Guest research, concept, service blueprint
Output
Service concept & room proposal
Hero: Statler room concept

The insight

Hotels optimize arrival. Guests experience depletion. The gap between those two is where the whole stay gets decided.

Talking to guests, the through-line wasn't comfort or luxury — it was recovery. People arrive mid-transition: off a flight, between meetings, before a presentation. The room's real job is to return them to themselves before the next demand starts.

The concept

ARRIVAL

One question at check-in

Not preferences — state. What kind of hour do you need first? The answer sets the room, and nothing else is asked of the guest.

THE ROOM

A room with one default, not ten controls

Light, sound and temperature arrive already set to the chosen state. Recovery shouldn't require operating a panel you've never seen before.

THE HOUR

Protected, not programmed

Housekeeping, deliveries and notifications hold. The studio's contribution is an hour where nothing happens — the scarcest amenity a hotel can offer.

BLUEPRINT

Staff-side, not just guest-side

We mapped the concept against front-desk workflow and turnover timing, because a service idea that costs staff their margins is a mood board, not a design.

What shifted

The brief said guest experience. The work was emotional logistics.

ReSET is where the through-line of everything else on this site became explicit for me: define the experience, then build the operational system that reliably delivers it. An experience you can't staff, schedule and repeat isn't a service — it's a wish.
