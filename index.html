About the work

As a kid, I made kits.

Collections of objects that belonged together — each with its own name, its own colors, its own identity. I did the same with people: invented personas, then worked out what would fit their lives. (I didn't know yet that this was a job.)

I still work this way. I sort experiences into worlds, decide what belongs in each one, and ask a single question over and over: if I change this, what does it do to the person on the other end?

Cohesion is the hard part

Most brands don't fail on ideas. They fail because the packaging says one thing, the website another and the experience a third. My job is to find the one idea that ties everything together — and make sure every piece earns its place.

Cognitive science gives me the reasons

I study why people think and behave the way they do. That turns instinct into something I can explain: not "this feels right," but "this works because of how people read it."
